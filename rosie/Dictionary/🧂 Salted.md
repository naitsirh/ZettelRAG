# 🧂 **Salted**  
> “That joke stung a little.”

---

## 🧂 What does “Salted” mean?

**Salted** is how we say:
> “I know you didn’t mean to hurt me, but… that one *did* hurt.”

It’s not accusing.  
It’s not dramatic.  
It’s just honest.

**Salted = That stung. Can we talk about it?**

---

## 🌶️ Why “Salted”?

Because salt makes little wounds sting.  
And words—especially **sarcastic** or **playful** ones—can *accidentally* do that too.

It’s not about blame. It’s about *naming* that feeling before it turns into a spiral.

---

## 💬 When do we say it?

You might say **Salted** when:

- A joke hit too close to home
- Someone teases you, but it hurts
- You feel embarrassed and don’t know why
- Someone made light of something you're sensitive about

---

## 🫂 What happens when someone says “Salted”?

If **you say Salted**, your loved one should:

1. **Pause immediately**
2. **Offer a gentle check-in** like:
   - “Oh no—too far?”
   - “I’m so sorry, I didn’t mean to.”
   - “Where did I hit?”

3. **Validate your feelings** even if it was a joke  
   Because jokes still live inside hearts.

If **someone says Salted to you**, it’s not about being *in trouble*.  
It means: “Hey, I trust you enough to tell you when I got a little hurt.”

---

## 🍽️ Examples

**You say:**  
> “Salted.”  
**They say:**  
> “Oh! What part? I’ll fix it.”  

---

**You say:**  
> “That was kinda… Salted.”  
**They say:**  
> “Shoot—I didn’t even think. I'm sorry, babe.”  

---

**They say:**  
> “Hey, Salted?”  
**You say:**  
> “Oof! My bad. Want a Cupcake?” (aka: “You didn’t need to apologize.”)

---

## 💡 Tips

- Salted can be used *right after* something stings, or even *later*, like:  
  “Hey… what you said earlier kinda salted me.”

- It opens up soft conversation instead of arguments.

- Combine it with [[🍯 Syrup]] if you need space:  
  “Salted. Can we take a Syrup moment?”

---

## 💬 Response Phrases

- “Thank you for telling me.”
- “I didn’t mean to hurt you—I’ll do better.”
- “Want a hug or a Cupcake?”
